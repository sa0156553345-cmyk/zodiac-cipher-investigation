# Report on the search for the original source of the Phillips 66 map and its calibration

## Summary

I have not yet found a primary or institutional source that identifies the **edition** of the Phillips 66 map that the Zodiac sent, its **date**, or the **documented scale** of the section "San Francisco and vicinity". Therefore **I cannot perform an independent geographic calibration from the original map, and I do not claim reliable control points**.

**Verdict: we are still at the exploration stage.** The geographic basis of the original map is not yet established.

## 2. What was rejected, and what cannot serve as the original map

I rejected every map that was not produced by Phillips 66 or does not match the edition year (S10 to S14 and S16). These include Shell, Gousha and Standard Oil maps from 1934 to 1964, and a map at 1:950,400 with no named publisher. These are not the original map, even if they look similar. Rejecting them here is consistent with your condition.

## 3. What is missing for a reliable calibration

1. **The original source itself:** a high-resolution image (600 dpi or more) of the section sent by the Zodiac, or of a matching copy of the Phillips 66 edition for "San Francisco and vicinity", with the printing code and the edition date. Likely places: the full FBI files (parts 02 to 06, not yet read), the San Francisco Chronicle archive for June 1970, high-resolution images on zodiackiller.com, or a physical copy of the Phillips 66 map from a large map library.
2. **Edition verification:** a cataloguing record for the Phillips 66 California map dated 1969 or 1970, with a statement of scale. The two conflicting claims (1969 against a scale of 6.4 with no date) need at least one source each.
3. **A documented scale:** a scale bar, or a statement "1 inch = ... miles" printed on the same section, or a measurement of a known distance (for example, the width of the area) on the original map.
4. **Control points:** once the original map is obtained, unambiguous features are chosen (airports, bridges, specific road junctions, the shoreline of the bay) and matched with their modern coordinates from an independent source. Written city names are not sufficient.
5. **Verification of the marks:** whether the circles and the numbers 0, 3, 6 and 9 exist on the original map, and where they lie relative to the centre of the crosshair.

## 4. A test that can be run as soon as the original scale is available

- The figure published by Foxon covers about **39 miles** horizontally at the scale calculated earlier (916 pixels ÷ 23.5 pixels per mile). If the map's scale were 6.4 miles per inch, the section shown in the image would be only about **6.1 inches** of the printed section.
- If the Zodiac section was **8 inches** wide, as the publication states, the image would cover about **51 miles** at 6.4, or about **4.9 miles per inch** if the image covers the whole section. These figures settle nothing until it is verified that the image represents the whole section, without enlargement.
- **Weighting criterion:** if the original source measures the width of the section in inches and counts the miles it covers, the result gives the printed scale. Either 6.4, or the value of the circles (1.09 miles per unit, if one circle unit is a printed inch), is tested against it.

## 5. What we cannot establish now

- The edition and date of the map.
- The printed scale, including whether 6.4 is correct.
- The existence of matching control points between the original map and the modern map.
- That the Foxon image represents the section sent, without enlargement or cropping.
- The correctness of the printing code 9-QQ-1219-S, or of the description of the 1969 edition.

## 6. Where we stop

Under your condition 6, I do not give any proposed coordinates for the Zodiac, and I do not use the proposed location or Route 24 as evidence. The next stage requires the original map first, not further calculations on the Foxon image.
